Are Banks Open on Presidents Day 2027?

No. Monday, 15 February 2027 is Washington's Birthday, the holiday most people call Presidents Day, and it is on the Federal Reserve Board's K.8 list of holidays observed by the Federal Reserve System. The Reserve Banks are closed, so ACH batches, Fedwire transfers and check clearing do not settle that Monday.

The holiday is fixed by law as the third Monday in February. February 2027 starts on a Monday, so the first Monday is the 1st, the second is the 8th, and the third is the 15th. In 2026 the date was Monday, 16 February, which is why a calendar carried over from last year will be off by a day.

Last and next settlement days

Friday, 12 February 2027 is the last Reserve Bank business day before the long weekend. Anything that has to land before the holiday, such as a payroll run, a loan payment or an outgoing wire, should be sent so it settles on that Friday. Saturday the 13th and Sunday the 14th are ordinary weekend days, and settlement resumes on Tuesday, 16 February.

What still works

Debit and credit cards, ATMs and online banking normally stay up, because they run on bank and card-network systems rather than on the Reserve Banks' calendar. You can usually see your balance and withdraw cash that is already in the account. What does not happen is interbank settlement: a transfer to another bank or a mobile check deposit made on Monday will normally start clearing on Tuesday. Many branches close for the day, but hours vary by bank, so check with your own branch before making a trip.

Stock markets

The New York Stock Exchange lists Washington's Birthday, Monday, 15 February 2027, as a market holiday too, so both the payment system and the main US equity market are shut on the same day.

The rest of early 2027

Before February, January 2027 has two Federal Reserve holidays: New Year's Day on Friday, 1 January, and Martin Luther King Jr. Day on Monday, 18 January. After Presidents Day, the next K.8 holiday is Memorial Day on Monday, 31 May 2027. March and April have no Federal Reserve holidays; Good Friday, 26 March, closes the NYSE but not the Reserve Banks.
